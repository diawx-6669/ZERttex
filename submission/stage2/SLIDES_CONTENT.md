## Slide 2 · The problem (0:45)

**Headline:** Foreign guests leave before they pay

**On the slide**
- 3,400 travelers in 2025, about 96% from Kazakhstan [Case fact]
- Sales by Instagram, WhatsApp and phone; payment by Kaspi or cash; a one-page Russian website [Case fact]
- What foreign guests said [Case fact]:
  - "It is unclear how to book."
  - "I am not sure the company is trustworthy."
  - "My foreign card does not work."
  - "I do not know what is included."

**Speaker notes**
"The company is good at local trips, but every step of its sales process assumes a local customer. The case gives us four sentences from foreign guests. Each one stops a booking before the payment: they cannot see what is included, they cannot check the company, and when they finally decide, their card does not work. To reach the goal, the company needs about 1,263 more foreign travelers per year. Fixing one of these complaints is not enough. We need to fix all four in one flow."

## Slide 4 · The solution: one closed loop (0:45)

**Headline:** Discover, plan, verify, book, travel, share, and back to discover

**On the slide**
- Discover: tour pages in English and 中文 with four lists: included, optional, not included, not yet confirmed
- Plan: a rules-based trip builder that fits real tour lengths and transfers
- Verify: a Trust Center (guide video, a call before paying, refund rules) and a concierge that hands hard questions to a person
- Book: three steps, foreign card on the payment provider's page (TEST MODE in the prototype)
- Travel: My trip with check-ins and reschedule requests
- Share: a postcard with a real QR code that brings the next traveler back
- Every claim carries one of five labels: Case fact · Verified source · Proposal · Demo data · Needs confirmation

**Speaker notes**
"Our product is one loop, not a list of features. Each step removes one doubt, and the last step, a postcard sized for Xiaohongshu and WeChat, brings a friend back to the first step. We also show what we do not know yet. Park fees, the Kaindy 4x4 transfer and a Chinese-speaking guide on a given date sit in a separate 'not yet confirmed' list. We believe honesty builds more trust than a perfect-looking page. Let us show you."

## Slide 6 · Why not just Trip.com or GetYourGuide? (0:35)

**Headline:** Big platforms help, but they should not be the only channel

**On the slide**
- GetYourGuide charges suppliers a 20–30% commission, depending on the country of the activity (GetYourGuide supplier page, accessed 27 Oct 2026) [Verified source]
- On a 44,000 KZT trip that is 8,800–13,200 KZT per traveler, close to our whole 13,200 KZT margin assumption [Case fact + Proposal]
- A marketplace listing shows a product, not the company: no guide video, no "not yet confirmed" list, no relationship with the traveler
- Our plan: own direct channel first; a Trip.com / Klook listing later as an extra channel [Proposal]

**Speaker notes**
"A fair question is: why build anything, when Trip.com and GetYourGuide already sell tours here? They solve payment, but the commission is 20 to 30 percent according to GetYourGuide itself. With an average price of 44,000 tenge, that can take most of the company's margin on each traveler. And the traveler meets a listing, not a company. We keep the platforms as a later, additional channel, but the direct channel is where the margin and the trust stay."

## Likely jury questions (short answers)

1. **"Is the AI concierge real or scripted?"**
   Both. In the MVP the concierge is scripted and always works. When our AI server is connected, the app shows "Live AI · beta" and answers come from Claude through our server, using only the verified tour data; every AI answer is marked as AI-generated. If the AI fails or takes more than 8 seconds, the app silently uses the scripted answer. On stage we can switch to scripted mode with one click.

2. **"Where do 1,263 travelers come from?"**
   About 4% of 3,400 travelers are foreign today, roughly 136. If the company keeps its 3,264 local travelers, 30% foreign means about 4,663 in total, so about 1,399 foreign travelers. That is about 1,263 more than today.

3. **"Why is the margin 30%?"**
   It is our assumption, not a case fact. The company did not publish its costs. The sheet shows payback from 20% to 40%: month 46 to month 27 in the base case. The first thing we would do with the company is replace it with real cost data.

4. **"Can a foreign card really be accepted by a Kazakh company?"**
   We plan to use a payment provider's hosted checkout for international cards, so card data never reaches our servers. Which provider, its fees and its terms still need confirmation; in the prototype all payments are in TEST MODE.

5. **"What if the pilot fails?"**
   Then we do not release Gate 2. The company has spent 14.5 million instead of 20 million and keeps reusable assets: bilingual tour pages, guide videos and a direct booking channel that also works for local customers.

6. **"How do you stop the AI from inventing prices or availability?"**
   It only receives the verified data from the app, with its labels. Its rules forbid prices, availability, licences and safety guarantees that are not in that data, and it must hand the question to a manager instead. The server also re-checks every answer and drops unknown labels and routes. Managers must edit and approve every AI reply draft; nothing is sent automatically.

7. **"Did you talk to real Chinese travelers?"**
   Not yet at the time of the pitch. [Update if interviews are done before 29 October.] Interviews are week 1–2 of the plan, and the app has an empty slot for real quotes: we will not show any quote we did not collect.

8. **"Why not just list the tours on Trip.com?"**
   Commission is 20–30% (GetYourGuide's own figure for its platform), which can take most of the margin, and a listing does not build trust in the company. We add marketplaces later as an extra channel.
